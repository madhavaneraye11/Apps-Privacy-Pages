KidSpark Privacy Policy

Effective date: 17 October 2026 · App: KidSpark (com.maddytech.kidspark) · Developer: Madhav Aneraye

In short
  • KidSpark has no accounts, no sign-in, no ads and does not use the advertising ID.
  • Your child's name, photos, voice recordings and drawings stay on your phone. We never receive them.
  • If you allow it, the app sends anonymous crash reports and feature-use counts to help us fix and improve it. You can switch this off in the Parent Zone.
  • Purchases are handled entirely by Google Play.

Who we are

KidSpark is a learning app for children aged 2 to 8, made by Madhav Aneraye ("we", "us"). It is designed to be used by a child together with a parent or guardian ("grown-up"). If you have any question about this policy, contact us at maddytech11@gmail.com.

Information kept only on your phone

To make the app personal, KidSpark stores the following in the app's private storage on your device. It is not sent to us or to anyone else.

WhatWhy
Each child's first name or nickname, age, chosen avatar and languageTo greet the child and choose lessons that suit their age
Progress, stars, streaks, stickers, certificates and settingsTo show progress and rewards and remember choices
Photos and names a grown-up adds in "My Family"So the child can learn the names of their own people and things
Voice recordings a grown-up makes (Voice Studio, My Family, story reading)So the app can speak in a familiar voice
Drawings the child makesTo keep them in the child's art gallery

Photos are chosen with Android's photo picker, so KidSpark only sees the photos you pick, never your whole gallery. The microphone is used only when a grown-up taps "Record" in an area protected by the parental gate. Recordings are limited to a few seconds each.

Information sent off your phone

1. Anonymous diagnostics (Firebase, by Google) — only while "Help improve KidSpark" is on (Parent Zone → Privacy). It is on by default and you can turn it off at any time; collection stops immediately.

  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics): the technical details of an error, the device model, Android version, app version, and the child's age group (toddler, preschool or school age), plan (free or Plus) and second language.
  • Feature use (Firebase Analytics): which kind of activity was finished, for example "a lesson in Animals was completed" or "a quiz got 3 stars", plus the same age group, plan, language and number of child profiles.
  • Firebase also uses a random app-installation ID and, like any internet service, receives your device's IP address to deliver the data. Google's Firebase privacy information: firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

These diagnostics never include names, photos, recordings, drawings, anything a child types, contacts, location or the advertising ID. We have turned off advertising features, ad personalisation and data sharing with Google for advertising. We use this information only to find and fix problems and to understand which parts of the app are useful.

2. Purchases (Google Play) — KidSpark Plus is bought through Google Play. Google handles payment details under the Google Privacy Policy. We never see your card or payment details; the app only receives confirmation from Google Play that Plus is active on this account.

3. Backup to your Google account — If Android backup is turned on for your phone, Android may back up KidSpark's settings and progress (which include each child's name and age) to your Google account, so they come back if you reinstall or change phones. We cannot access this backup. Family photos, voice recordings and drawings are excluded from this cloud backup.

4. Things you choose to share — When a grown-up taps Share, Save or Print (for example a certificate, drawing, worksheet or report card), the picture or PDF goes only to the app or printer you pick, such as WhatsApp, or to your phone's Pictures/KidSpark folder. These options are behind the parental gate.

5. Speech — When no recording exists, KidSpark reads words aloud using your phone's text-to-speech engine. Some engines, such as Google's, may use the internet for certain voices under their own privacy policy.

What we do not do

  • No advertising and no advertising ID.
  • No selling or renting of any information.
  • No location, contacts, camera or phone-number access.
  • No chat, social features or links that let a child contact other people.
  • No collection of personal information from children by us.

Children's privacy

KidSpark is built for children and follows the Google Play Families Policy. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Information that identifies a child stays on the device. Settings, purchases, sharing, recording and adding photos are behind a parental gate meant for grown-ups. This approach is intended to meet the requirements of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and the GDPR.

How long information is kept, and deleting it

  • On your phone: until you delete it. Remove a child in Parent Zone → Children to delete their profile, progress and drawings; delete recordings and photos in Voice Studio and My Family; or uninstall the app to remove everything, including recorded story readings.
  • Diagnostics: Firebase keeps crash reports for about 90 days, and we keep analytics data for the shortest period Firebase allows. Because this data is anonymous, we cannot link it to you or your child. To ask for it to be deleted, turn diagnostics off and email us.

Security

Data on your phone is kept in KidSpark's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Diagnostics are sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS).

Your choices and rights

You can turn diagnostics on or off, delete any child's information, or uninstall the app at any time. Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal data, or to complain to a data protection authority. Contact us and we will help.

Changes to this policy

If we change how KidSpark handles information, we will update this page and the effective date above, and tell you about important changes in the app.
